Long

Pascaline Wolfermann is a global healthcare intelligence and aeromedical consultant with more than 30 years of experience in international health assistance, medical logistics and travel medicine. She is the founder of Pelagos Global, an independent practice helping insurers, assistance companies, employers, universities and NGOs protect people who live, work and study far from home. From 2016 to 2025 she was Director, Global Solutions Healthcare Intelligence at UnitedHealthcare Global, where she led the global medical and transport provider quality assessment program and country health surveillance that briefed globally mobile customers on the healthcare landscape, access and quality at their destination. Her team developed customized health intelligence reports, including Medical Emergency Response Plans for large events and remote locations. Before that she directed health, aeromedical and logistics intelligence for UnitedHealth Group’s global assistance business and spent nearly a decade in resource development at FrontierMEDEX. Her career began in 1993 as an administrator and logistician with Médecins du Monde during the cholera outbreak in Iquitos, Peru. Pascaline chairs the Ethics Committee of the European Aero-Medical Institute (EURAMI), served on its board from 2012 to 2018, and co-authored a 2025 Air Medical Journal article on the ethics of cross-border aeromedical transport. She studied international relations and languages at Université Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne.

Available talks

Talk topics

Each topic can be tailored to your audience, from boards and HR leaders to clinicians, operators and students.

01

Ethics in cross-border aeromedical transport

When a patient flies across borders, clinical, legal and commercial interests can pull in different directions. Drawing on her EURAMI Ethics Committee work and the 2025 Air Medical Journal article she co-authored, Pascaline explores consent, transport to or from treatments prohibited at home, and how providers and payers can make defensible decisions.

02

Medical emergency response planning for mass gatherings

Large events and remote venues concentrate risk where local care may be thin. A practical walk-through of how a Medical Emergency Response Plan is built: mapping capable facilities, transport options and escalation paths before the first attendee arrives.

03

Duty of care for globally mobile people

Employees, students and volunteers abroad deserve to know what care looks like where they are going. How country health intelligence and pre-assignment briefings turn duty of care from a policy into real preparedness.

04

Assessing medical & transport provider quality worldwide

Not every hospital or air ambulance is what its brochure promises. Lessons from leading a global provider quality assessment program: what to measure, how to verify it across very different health systems, and how to keep a network current.

05

Lessons from field logistics in the Amazon cholera response

Pascaline’s career began in Iquitos, Peru, as a Médecins du Monde logistician during a cholera outbreak in the Amazon basin. A reflective talk on what frontline humanitarian logistics teaches about access, improvisation and the human side of global health assistance.
