How I can help

Six situations where three decades of international health assistance, aeromedical and medical-logistics work make a practical difference: from the first briefing to a plan your team can actually run.

Illustrative scenarios based on the kinds of work Pascaline has led. They are not client case studies.

Client type

Global employer sending staff to remote mining sites

The challenge

Teams rotate into sites hours from the nearest capable hospital. Site managers need to know exactly who to call, where to go and how a patient leaves the country if something serious happens on a night shift.

Intended value

A response plan that is realistic for the location, understood on site and ready before the first incident.

The approach

  • Map the local healthcare landscape: access, capability and quality of nearby facilities
  • Assess medical and transport providers against clear quality criteria
  • Design escalation and evacuation routes, including air ambulance options
  • Build a site-specific Medical Emergency Response Plan with local teams

Deliverables

  • Medical Emergency Response Plan
  • Facility & provider map
  • Evacuation decision tree
Client type

University study-abroad office

The challenge

Students and faculty spread across many countries, each with different health systems. The office wants to brief travellers well, and to know how it would coordinate with assistance partners if unrest or illness struck.

Intended value

Better-prepared travellers and a clear, rehearsed role for the university when an emergency happens abroad.

The approach

  • Country health surveillance for each destination programme
  • Pre-departure briefings on local healthcare access and quality
  • Review of assistance and insurance arrangements against real scenarios
  • Crisis coordination roles between campus, programme staff and assistance providers

Deliverables

  • Destination health briefings
  • Duty-of-care gap review
  • Emergency coordination playbook
Client type

International health insurer / TPA

The challenge

A growing network of hospitals, clinics and transport vendors abroad, but uneven insight into which ones deliver consistent quality, and no common yardstick for deciding where to steer members.

Intended value

A defensible, repeatable way to choose and monitor providers, so members are directed to care you can stand behind.

The approach

  • Define quality criteria for medical and transport providers
  • Design an assessment programme modelled on the one she led at UnitedHealthcare Global
  • Prioritise countries and providers by member exposure and risk
  • Set up review cycles and escalation for quality concerns

Deliverables

  • Assessment framework
  • Provider scorecards
  • Network recommendations
Client type

Air ambulance operator preparing for accreditation

The challenge

The operator wants accreditation to build trust with assistance companies and insurers, but needs an outside view of where its policies, documentation and ethical safeguards stand today.

Intended value

A clear path to audit, with the ethical and operational foundations buyers of aeromedical services look for.

The approach

  • Gap review against EURAMI-style accreditation standards
  • Ethics and patient-consent practice in cross-border transport, drawing on her EURAMI Ethics Committee work
  • Medical escort and bed-to-bed process review
  • Positioning toward assistance companies and insurers

Deliverables

  • Readiness gap report
  • Prioritised action plan
  • Mock-audit walkthrough
Client type

Organizer of a large international event

The challenge

Thousands of delegates, athletes or guests in one city for a few days. Organizers must understand local hospital capacity, who handles what, and how international visitors are cared for and repatriated if needed.

Intended value

Confidence that medical coverage matches the event's scale, with local and international partners aligned beforehand.

The approach

  • Customised health intelligence report on the host city and venues
  • Assessment of receiving hospitals and transport capacity
  • Event Medical Emergency Response Plan, including mass-casualty escalation
  • Repatriation and assistance pathways for international attendees

Deliverables

  • Host-city health intelligence report
  • Event response plan
  • Partner contact matrix
Client type

NGO deploying to an outbreak zone

The challenge

A team must deploy quickly to a remote area during an outbreak, where supply chains are fragile and the local health system is under strain. Staff safety and field logistics have to work from day one.

Intended value

A deployment that protects staff, supports the response on the ground and has a credible plan for when things go wrong.

The approach

  • Field logistics planning informed by her Médecins du Monde cholera response work in the Amazon basin
  • Rapid country health surveillance and local provider mapping
  • Staff health, evacuation and medical escort arrangements
  • Coordination with assistance companies and local authorities

Deliverables

  • Deployment health brief
  • Field logistics plan
  • Staff evacuation protocol
